## Abstract

This paper proposes a hypothesis that India may effectively leverage its demographic dividend (Drishti IAS 2019) through a dual-track policy intervention combining Universal Basic Income (UBI) with the Heckman (2008) framework for early childhood care and education. India's demographic dividend window, extending from 2005 to 2055—now halfway through as of 2026—presents both opportunity and urgency. This study argues that such integrated policy interventions could generate superior economic returns compared to alternative approaches. Rather than presenting new empirical evidence, this work synthesizes existing literature to develop a testable hypothesis for subsequent rigorous investigation.

## Introduction

This paper develops a theoretical hypothesis regarding potential complementarities between a dual-track policy combining Universal Basic Income and early human capital investment, grounded in Heckman's (2008) framework on skill formation, and improvements in India's political economy. Recent scholarship identifies UBI as a policy priority for India (Singh 2026). Building on existing literature, this paper proposes a conceptual framework to guide subsequent rigorous empirical evaluation. Definitive assessment would require appropriately designed pilot programs implementing both UBI and early childhood human capital investment in India, coupled with rigorous impact evaluation methodology.

## Methodology

This study synthesizes existing literature on Universal Basic Income and early childhood care and education (ECCE) to develop an integrated theoretical framework. The paper hypothesizes that India could achieve substantial benefits from nationwide implementation of these complementary interventions at the grassroots level, with particular urgency given the finite window of the demographic dividend. Drawing on Drishti IAS (2019) and recent scholarship, this work posits—without generating new empirical evidence—that India would benefit significantly from integrating Universal Basic Income with Heckman's (2008) framework for early childhood care and education, implemented with appropriate urgency to capitalize on temporal constraints.

## Discussion and Conclusion

Evidence from existing literature suggests that India could achieve substantial development gains by prioritizing prenatal care and Universal Basic Income as complementary policy instruments. Early childhood interventions, particularly prenatal care, consistently demonstrate higher returns on investment than subsequent schooling or job training programs. Strategic implementation of these preventive measures could transform India's demographic potential from a manageable challenge into a catalyst for sustained economic development and human capital accumulation.
